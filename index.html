02

Harm reduction is home turf

My program experience is in harm reduction, and it informs how I approach technology. Small nonprofits often work with lean staffing and limited budgets, without a dedicated IT person. When a website breaks or a workflow falls apart, the people already doing frontline work are left carrying the technical problem, too.

I coordinated a mail-order syringe service program: requests came in, supplies were packed and shipped, and records had to stay organized. That experience helped me recognize the practical needs behind Harmonize Hub's order, inventory, and record workflows. The Community Alerts module is a design effort toward fast, organized communication. I have not operated a bad-batch alert system, and I don't claim that experience. My experience is in the program operations that surround this work, and in designing technology to support it.

If I take over your site, week one is an audit, not a redesign:

Week one — the audit

  • ✓
    Who has access to what — and who shouldn't anymore
  • ✓
    Where the site lives: hosting, domain, how it's built

    Whether updates and backups are actually running
  • ✓
    Security basics: logins, roles, recovery
  • ✓
    Every page and form, checked for what's broken

Then it's steady care

Then it's steady care: updates kept current, content changes when staff need them, backups verified, and one person your team can call when something acts up.
